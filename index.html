The Big Bang Theory

The universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from an infinitely hot, dense singularity. In the first fractions of a second, space itself expanded faster than light (cosmic inflation), seeding the structures we see today.

13.8 ByrAge
10⁻³⁶ sInflation

Cosmic Microwave Background

The afterglow of the Big Bang — a nearly uniform bath of microwave radiation at 2.725 K, discovered in 1965 by Penzias & Wilson. Tiny fluctuations (1 part in 100,000) map the seeds of galaxies.

1965Discovered
COBE/WMAP/PlanckMissions
🕳️

Dark Matter

An invisible substance making up ~27% of the universe. It doesn't emit or absorb light but reveals itself through gravity — holding galaxies together and bending light (gravitational lensing).

27%Of Universe
WIMPs/AxionsCandidates

Dark Energy

A mysterious force making up ~68% of the universe, responsible for its accelerating expansion. Discovered in 1998 through observations of distant Type Ia supernovae — Nobel Prize 2011.

68%Of Universe
1998Discovered

Black Holes

Regions where gravity is so strong that nothing — not even light — can escape. First imaged in 2019 (M87*) and 2022 (Sagittarius A*). Range from stellar-mass to supermassive (billions of solar masses).

Ultra-dense remnants of supernovae — a sugar-cube of neutron star material weighs a billion tons. Pulsars are rapidly rotating neutron stars emitting beams of radiation like cosmic lighthouses.

Quasars

The brightest objects in the universe — supermassive black holes actively feeding, outshining entire galaxies. Visible across billions of light-years. First identified in 1963 (3C 273).

Supernovae

Colossal stellar explosions marking the death of massive stars or the thermonuclear detonation of white dwarfs. They forge heavy elements (gold, uranium) and seed the cosmos with the building blocks of life.

Aryabhata (476–550 CE)

India's greatest ancient astronomer. In his Aryabhatiya, he proposed Earth's rotation, calculated the sidereal year as 365.258 days, explained eclipses as shadows, and gave π ≈ 3.1416.

Brahmagupta (598–668 CE)

Author of Brahmasphutasiddhanta. Described gravity as an attractive force centuries before Newton, calculated Earth's circumference within 1% of modern value, and formalized zero.

628 CEMasterwork

MIND-BENDING FACTS

The Universe in Numbers

Some facts that will change how you see the night sky.

💡

Neutron Star Density

A teaspoon of neutron star material weighs about 6 billion tons — roughly the weight of Mount Everest compressed into a spoon.

⏱️

Time Dilation

Time runs slower in stronger gravity. Near a black hole's event horizon, hours can equal decades elsewhere — confirmed by GPS satellites daily.

🌌

Observable Universe

The observable universe is 93 billion light-years across, contains ~2 trillion galaxies, and ~10²⁴ stars — more than grains of sand on all Earth's beaches.

🔇

Silent Universe

Space is a vacuum — no sound can travel. The "sounds" of space you hear are electromagnetic waves converted to audio by scientists.

🌠

Looking Back in Time

Every time you look at a star, you see it as it was in the past. The Andromeda Galaxy appears as it was 2.5 million years ago.

🕳️

Spaghettification

Falling into a black hole, tidal forces stretch you into a long thin strand — literally like spaghetti. A real astrophysical term!

🌞

Sun's Power

Every second, the Sun converts 4 million tons of matter into energy via nuclear fusion. It has been doing this for 4.6 billion years.

🪐

Exotic Weather

On Neptune, it rains diamonds. On Venus, it rains sulfuric acid. On Titan, it rains liquid methane. Weather gets weirder the farther you go.

🌍

India's Cost-Effective Space

Mangalyaan cost just $74 million — less than the budget of the movie Gravity. ISRO is famous for world-class science at a fraction of global costs.

⚛️

Empty Space

If you removed all the empty space from atoms in every human on Earth, the entire population would fit inside a sugar cube. Matter is 99.9999999% empty space.

🌊

Great Attractor

A gravitational anomaly 250 million light-years away pulling our galaxy and 100,000 others toward it at 600 km/s. We can't see it — it's hidden behind the Milky Way's zone of avoidance.

🔭

Fast Radio Bursts

Mysterious millisecond bursts of radio energy from deep space. Some repeat, some don't. India's GMRT has been key in detecting and studying them.
